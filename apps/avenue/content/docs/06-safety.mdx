## Safety and driver distraction

A whole-car system puts a screen in front of the driver, so distraction is the first question. AVENUE's driver mode is designed to the U.S. National Highway Traffic Safety Administration's visual-manual driver distraction guidelines. These guidelines are **voluntary**; they apply to light vehicles and to built-in devices the driver can easily see or reach.

### What the driver's screen does while moving

- **Locks** video not related to driving, automatically scrolling text, text entry, long text for reading (news bodies), and camera imagery (the AR view).
- **Enlarges** targets to 76 px and cuts the screen to two glanceable tiles: the next turn and what is playing.
- **Hands tasks** to voice ("warmer", "navigate home"), to the steering-wheel and seat controls (card A), and to other seats: a film the driver picks is sent to a rear screen instead.
- **Says why** something is locked ("available when parked"), so it is never a dead end.

Try it in the demo: choose the driver's seat and set the car to **Moving**.

### Status

These are design decisions, **not test results**. The driver mode has not been measured against the glance criteria; that test (eye-glance or occlusion, as the guidelines describe) is part of any pilot. Rear screens sit behind the front seats and are outside the guidelines' scope.
