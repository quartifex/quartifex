Interfaces poured from light.

Lumorphism is a design language where every surface, and every headline, is a glowing gel. A control's state is its mix: more glow pigment when it's live, more black when it rests.

Six rules for a material that glows.

Everything is poured

Shapes are puddles with a raised, rounded edge and a flat top. No hairline borders, no flat fills: thickness and gloss say where a surface starts.

State is the mix

Never swap colours to show state. Live, hovered and pressed controls carry more glow pigment; resting ones less; disabled ones are nearly all black.

Light spills

A glowing control lights the surface around it, and its light lands on the controls beside it, catching their gloss and rims. Focus is a brighter spill, not a ring.

Related things merge

Grouped controls touch and flow into one body. A toggle's drop slides through its track; a slider's fill and handle are one pour.

Two rooms, one material

Light mode and dark mode are the room's lights, not two palettes. The gel is the same; in the dark the glow carries the interface.

Gel is thick

A press spreads the gel and sends a ripple across it; it settles at the speed its viscosity allows. Viscosity is a token, from water to honey.

One model, many liquids.

Glow is only one dial. Turn it off and the gel is a liquid; thin the pigment and it turns clear; scatter the light and it turns to milk; mirror the room and it is mercury. Each pour below is the same material with different dials, whatever the page is set to.

Glow gel

Emits its own light, which spills onto everything near it.

Liquid

Glow at zero: a coloured liquid that only shows the light it is given.

Clear water

Almost no pigment: it bends what is behind it and focuses light into its shadow.

Tinted syrup

Thin colour, thick refraction, the warm look of honey or glass.

Milk

Light scatters inside it, so it is soft, bright and opaque.

Mercury

A liquid mirror of the room and the surface around it.

How pours meet.

Drag a drop into another and fling it across. Merging drops pull together, join and mix their colour and material; bouncing ones push off each other at the page's bounce.
